# Local model release ewt-1

These are compact discriminative models. They label tokens, dependencies, and bounded error candidates; they do not generate prose.

| Component | Training and evaluation | Measured result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| POS | Word embeddings, character CNN, bidirectional LSTM, linear-chain CRF; 12 epochs; validation checkpoint selection | 94.47% token accuracy, 24,859 held-out tokens |
| Dependency parser | Arc-standard transition MLP; beam 4; 6 epochs; predicted POS; projective oracle training | UAS 81.66%, LAS 77.27%, 1,962 held-out sentences |

Neither result establishes natural spelling or grammar correction quality, and the candidate is not promoted.

## Independent learner-writing benchmark

The fixed production-browser evaluation in [`reports/realec-independent-evaluation.json`](../reports/realec-independent-evaluation.json) analyzes 184 held-out documents from the manually annotated REALEC learner-essay corpus: 60,825 Unicode source tokens and 4,017 tokens overlapped by at least one error annotation. The worker marked 585 tokens, of which 110 overlapped an annotated error span. Token-level precision is 18.80% (95% Wilson interval 15.84–22.17%), recall is 2.74% (2.28–3.29%), F0.5 is 8.65%, and false-positive rate on unannotated tokens is 0.836% (0.765–0.914%). A 1,000-replicate document-cluster bootstrap gives precision 14.21–23.90%, recall 2.10–3.42%, F0.5 6.71–10.57%, and false-positive rate 0.697–1.007%. Compared with the previous frozen run, this version produced 213 fewer unannotated findings and 18 fewer true positives: precision rose 3.11 points, total token misclassification fell 0.32 points, and false-positive rate fell 0.375 points, while recall fell 0.45 points. The change accepts common British spelling variants, guards temporal “after/before/since that” adjuncts from fragment review, anchors fragment findings to the subordinating marker, excludes relative “that” from parsed agreement, and expands curated direct misspelling corrections. The recall decrease partly reflects corpus annotations that treat valid British spellings as errors and narrower fragment spans that no longer overlap unrelated annotations. The poor recall and precision remain clear evidence that current coverage is inadequate for broad learner writing.

For the requested `<0.5%` limits, total token misclassification is `(FP + FN) / all tokens` and missed-error rate is `FN / (TP + FN)`; the latter must be below 0.5%, so recall must exceed 99.5%. On this fixed split, total token misclassification is 7.204% (4,382/60,825), missed-error rate is 97.26% (3,907/4,017), and false-positive rate is 0.836% (475/56,808). Meeting the three limits on this sample would allow at most 304 total token mistakes, 20 missed annotated-error tokens, and 284 false-positive tokens; the measured worker has 4,382, 3,907, and 475 respectively. These targets are not met. This comparison uses corpus annotation spans as its reference and does not demonstrate those rates on valid controls or all natural English writing.

The latest explicit request is at least 50% precision, at most 5% total token misclassification, and a false-positive rate below 1.21%. This run passes the false-positive limit (0.836%) but not precision (18.80%) or total token error (7.204%). The report also computes the exact feasibility consequence: at exactly 50% precision, false positives equal true positives, so total token errors equal the 4,017 gold error tokens (6.604%) regardless of recall. The 5% limit therefore requires precision above 56.91% even at perfect recall, and 97.24% at 25% recall; the current run has 365 fewer true positives than false positives and needs 1,341 more net correct detections. The 50% precision request is necessary but not sufficient.

This is a document-held-out sample from English essays written by Russian L1 university students; the source does not provide author or prompt identity, so documents may share writers. The report gives per-annotation-label token recall for triage, but labels are not equivalent to Linguorithm's writing goals and the evaluator does not infer category precision. The benchmark is not representative of preschool through advanced secondary writers. It supplies no pattern-specific valid controls or adjudicated correction judgments, so it cannot satisfy per-goal quality or direct-correction gates. The fixed test split is evaluation-only and is not used to train or tune production behavior. Source text and derived documents remain in the ignored project-local cache; the tracked report contains aggregate counts and label names only. REALEC is distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0; retain its attribution and share-alike terms for any redistributed derivatives ([official corpus page](https://rpractice.itorubarov.me/about/)).

The label breakdown shows where current coverage fails: high-volume discourse annotations matched 8/356 labeled tokens (2.25%), word-choice annotations 4/251 (1.59%), article-choice annotations 3/285 (1.05%), and punctuation annotations 3/161 (1.86%). Spelling is the strongest listed group at 40/148 tokens (27.03%), still far below a useful detector target. These are exploratory recall counts from one corpus, not validated per-goal scores.

## Expanded-corpus training candidate (2026-10-25)

The latest candidate trained the POS tagger and transition parser on the original EWT training split plus three additional licensed UD English training splits: Atis, CHILDES, and ESLSpok. After exact sentence deduplication, the supplemental splits contribute 39,545 sentences and 281,413 tokens; the pinned source files contain 40,862 sentences before filtering. EWT validation and test remain unchanged. The seven additional UD treebanks contribute their official test partitions to the supplemental evaluation; because three of their training partitions were used for fitting, this is split-held-out rather than fully source-held-out evaluation. Only CTeTex, LittlePrince, PUD, and Pronouns were held out by source. The aggregate supplemental-test report covers 100,028 tokens and is dominated by CHILDES (52,497 tokens); per-source results and token-level Wilson intervals are in [`reports/secondary-ud-evaluation.json`](../reports/secondary-ud-evaluation.json). These tests measure transfer among UD treebanks, not learner writing.

On the unchanged full EWT test (1,962 sentences; 24,859 tokens), the candidate measured 94.6579% POS (+0.1850 points), 82.4088% UAS (+0.7522), and 77.9677% LAS (+0.6960) against the frozen release. Its exact packaged browser evaluation is preserved in [`reports/candidate-generalization-browser.json`](../reports/candidate-generalization-browser.json): 93.4135% POS (−0.0820 points), 82.0443% UAS (+0.1366), and 77.5348% LAS (−0.2732) on the same 300 sentences and 3,659 gold tokens. That report also records 55/63 synthetic agreement mutations detected with one false positive, versus the frozen path's 53/63 with none, and a failed source/edit-integrity case for quoted speech. This archived report predates a later modal-chain fix and includes a false positive for valid passive “will be examined”; the updated rule abstains when auxiliary evidence is unstable and has a cross-browser regression. The candidate therefore fails the browser no-regression and integrity gates and was not promoted; the frozen archive remains active. These are small within-corpus syntax gains, not the required +5-point improvements or independent learner-writing validation. Candidate feature parity and Python/browser classifier score parity pass, with the latter covering 20 fixtures and 20 classifier tokens at a maximum difference of 0.00000191.

The licensed English UD inventory is finite: eight English treebanks are used here, with the other active official treebanks screened for license, split, and content suitability. This cannot yield one hundred times as many distinct UD sources, and near-duplicate files are not counted as new sources. The Open American National Corpus adds 6,424 written documents and 13,738,685 tokens only to candidate word/bigram context counts for ranking; it supplies no dependency labels or learner-error labels. Its spoken collections are excluded. Source details, pins, and limitations are in the [dataset expansion research note](dataset-expansion-research-2026-10-25.md).

## Earlier parser-only candidate investigation (2026-10-25)

A parser candidate selected on the official development split was evaluated once on each unchanged held-out test. The full EWT result was POS 94.4728% (unchanged), UAS 82.2117% (+0.5551 percentage points), and LAS 77.7344% (+0.4626 points) on 1,962 sentences / 24,859 tokens. On the fixed browser path it was POS 92.1837% (unchanged), UAS 80.4865% (+0.3280 points), and LAS 76.2777% (+0.1913 points) on 300 sentences / 3,659 gold tokens. This does not meet the requested +5-point floor for any sub-93 metric. The shipped weights remain unchanged.

A richer parser with additional contextual features first reached 82.5950% UAS and 78.3846% LAS on development data. A fixed continuation then selected epoch 25 at 82.6349% UAS and 78.4686% LAS (20,667/25,010 heads and 19,625/25,010 labeled heads), only +0.0400/+0.0840 points over the earlier richer checkpoint. Epoch 30 declined despite lower training loss. This continuation read train/dev only, evaluated only at the predeclared epochs, and did not read or infer on test/browser data. Its 18-position parser interface differs from production's 10-position interface, and it has not been integrated, parity-checked, or evaluated on held-out data; it is not a release candidate. More epochs under this schedule are not a useful next step. In the earlier tokenizer run used for the parser investigation, neural span coverage was 3,561/3,659 tokens (97.32%); that historical coverage has since improved to 3,610/3,659 (98.66%). Even with the refreshed coverage, the current browser POS result remains 3.69 points below its minimum target. Local continuation inputs, source, candidates, and full logs remain in ignored `.cache/syntax-experiment/`; they did not modify the production bundle or fixed reports.

Pinned data: UD English EWT r2.16, commit `4c89b5833a70aa5ed3a00bad2f23f57992cc7df8`, CC-BY-SA 4.0. Source files and SHA-256 provenance are in `training/datasets/ewt-manifest.json`. Official document splits are retained and exact duplicate sentences excluded; author/prompt independence is unavailable. Training split: 12,542 sentences; validation: 1,933; test: 1,962. Syntax sequences are limited to 128 tokens; longer sentences use fallback analysis. Nonprojective training trees without valid oracle derivations are excluded (287 sentences).

The dictionary manifest pins its upstream commit and records composite permissive notices, including SCOWL, WordNet, VarCon, and Ispell. Preserve `apps/web/public/models/licenses/` when distributing the build. Do not relabel the dictionary as solely MIT. Corpus/derived-resource attribution and share-alike requirements remain applicable; the application’s own source is separate.

Every registry resource and model weight is checked with SHA-256 before inference. Python versus browser ONNX emissions pass tolerance 0.0002 in Chromium, Firefox, WebKit, and mobile Chromium emulation. Training uses predicted syntax features, and the release pipeline verifies exact browser feature parity plus actual Python ONNX versus browser WASM classifier score parity before packaging. ## Production-browser generalization check

[The recorded report](../reports/generalization.json), generated on 2026-10-27, runs the actual production browser worker with model state `ready` on 411 cases. Its syntax fixture contains 300 official-test EWT sentences, selected by seeded SHA-256 ordering before inspecting predictions (seed 20261024). Exact token-sequence and document overlap with train/dev is excluded; sentences must align to original text and contain at most 128 tokens. This is held-out **within-corpus** evaluation: EWT was also used for model development and test reporting. It is neither a new independent dataset nor an error-annotated learner-essay benchmark; author/prompt independence is unverified. The five source genres follow official EWT sentence-ID prefixes.

| **All**      |   **300** |   **3,659** | **93.50%** | **81.91%** | **77.81%** |

These scores include punctuation and count unmatched spans and lexical-fallback tokens as incorrect. POS measures token labels; UAS measures dependency heads; LAS additionally requires the exact basic UD relation label. Exact sentence tokenization matches on 266/300 sentences. Neural spans align to 3,610/3,659 gold tokens (98.66%); conditional on those aligned tokens, POS/UAS/LAS are 94.76%/83.02%/78.86%. Those conditional scores exclude alignment failures and must be read with coverage. Without punctuation, all-gold scores are 92.69%/81.95%/77.29% on 3,214 tokens. Gold syntax is never supplied to inference. Learned syntax differs from rules-only output on 299/300 sentences; difference alone does not demonstrate an accuracy improvement. The fixed browser sample was inspected during tokenizer development, so this refreshed score is a reproducible regression measure, not an untouched confirmatory estimate.

No classifier category is activated by the current emission gate. The gold-guided synthetic agreement check selects one `is/are/was/were/has/have` number mutation per eligible source sentence, then sends only corrupted text through production inference. Across 63 mutation/control pairs, the complete hybrid has TP 53, FN 10, FP 0, TN 63: 84.13% target detection and 0/63 target false positives. Classifier-only emitted findings have TP 0, FN 63, FP 0, TN 63. These counts are after production diagnostic resolution, where an overlapping deterministic finding can suppress a model finding. Source controls can contain unrelated errors; this is target-token detection, not natural-essay precision or correction acceptability.

The separate manually authored development probes detect the expected target and offer its correction in 4/5 agreement, 3/4 verb-form, 2/4 usage, 0/1 boundary, and 0/1 pronoun cases. Among 26 authored clean cases, zero have conservative false positives and one has a review warning. The probes are not independently reviewed or representative of learner essays. All 411 cases pass source/edit integrity checks over 229 proposals. The report records zero calibrated and 191 uncalibrated review warnings; its confidence policy permits numeric synthetic-task probabilities only for the calibrated ASCII, tokenization-stable cohort with learned subject morphology. No natural-essay confidence or learning benefit is established, and this evaluation does not change model weights.

Int8 candidates reduce tagger weights from 2,628,302 to 919,585 bytes, parser from 1,383,856 to 350,284, and classifier from 908,987 to 231,256. On the full held-out syntax set, int8 POS is 94.46%, UAS 81.66%, LAS 77.30%. Float32 remains active because browser parity has been verified for that variant; quantization alone does not authorize promotion. See `reports/quantization-evaluation.json` and `reports/model-packaging.json`.

Known limits: web/edited-English domain shift, synthetic errors, imperfect tokenization of web identifiers, fallback analysis beyond the bounded neural sentence length, rare or unseen morphological paradigms, ambiguous pronouns/modifiers, and educational validity. A clean report cannot certify correctness. Parser accuracy does not establish error-detection precision. Neither a Grammarly comparison nor a student learning benefit has been demonstrated.
